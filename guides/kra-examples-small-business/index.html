KRA examples for employees in a small business

Key result areas for eight common roles, each with KPIs you can measure. Copy what fits, keep it to three to five per person, and change the numbers to suit your business.

KRA vs KPI: the difference

A KRA (key result area) is what someone is responsible for. A KPI (key performance indicator) is the number showing how it’s going. So “collections” is a KRA, and “90% of invoices collected within 30 days” is a KPI.

How to set KRAs for your team

  1. Start from the business goal. What has to happen this year? Work down to which role affects it.
  2. List what each role really answers for. Three to five things. If you’ve listed ten, you’ve described a job, not its priorities.
  3. Attach a number you can actually get. A KPI nobody can measure will turn into an argument.
  4. Write it with the person. They’ll tell you what’s in their control and what isn’t.
  5. Remove overlaps. If two people own it, nobody does.
  6. Review monthly, for twenty minutes. Yearly reviews are too late to help anyone.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring what’s easy instead of what matters. Calls made is easy. Conversations held is what matters.
  • Holding someone to a number they don’t control.
  • Linking pay to KPIs before anyone trusts them.
  • Writing “improve communication” as a KRA. Improve it how, and how would you tell?

Common questions

What are KRA examples for employees?

Common examples: sales revenue for a sales executive, complaint resolution for customer care, collections for accounts, on-time output for a supervisor. Each should have one to three measurable KPIs.

What are KRA and KPI in simple terms?

A KRA is what you’re responsible for. A KPI is the number that shows how you’re doing.

How many KRAs should a role have?

Three to five. More than that and nothing’s really a priority.

What are KRA examples for managers?

Team output, team quality, developing people and keeping good staff. For example: jobs done on time, rework rate, one person trained for each critical job, and staff retention.
